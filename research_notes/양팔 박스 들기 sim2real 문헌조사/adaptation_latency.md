# Adaptation to Unknown Friction/Contact and Actuator/Gripper Latency in RL Sim-to-Real Manipulation

Context: (a) dual-arm plate squeeze-lift of a box, PPO in Isaac Lab, memoryless 56-D MLP actor, privileged-DR critic (asymmetric actor-critic), planned 4-step observation-history ablation; (b) cube pick-and-place with a slow binary Franka gripper (0.45-0.9 s latency) where a "gripper busy" observation bit gave 2.4x faster learning and an emergent early-open behaviour.

Verification legend: **[V]** = title/authors/venue/key claim checked against a primary page during this session. **[M]** = well-known citation given from memory, not re-fetched this session (bibliographic details very likely correct; specific numbers not re-checked, so verify before quoting numbers).

## Q1. Adaptation to unknown friction/contact properties (RMA, history/recurrent vs DR, teacher-student, asymmetric AC, online sys-ID, explicit friction estimation, bimanual/squeeze grasps)

### Takeaway
The main routes to friction/contact adaptation are (i) plain DR with a robust memoryless policy, (ii) implicit adaptation from proprioceptive history (LSTM, RMA-style 2-stage teacher→history encoder), and (iii) explicit online estimation (UP-OSI, friction estimators). These are well established in locomotion and in-hand rotation. But I found **no paper that adapts contact friction in a bimanual squeeze/compression grasp with controlled real-world friction variation**. Bimanual sim-to-real RL papers (box lift, lid twisting, block assembly) rely on DR (sometimes plus sim auto-tuning) and do not run friction-adaptation ablations. The single most relevant controlled comparison (Peng et al. 2018) found that a feed-forward policy with a short observation history was barely better than a memoryless one (0.70 vs 0.67 real success), while an LSTM reached 0.89. This is a direct warning for the planned 4-step-history ablation.

### Cited Findings

**Adaptation backbone papers**
- **RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots**: A. Kumar, Z. Fu, D. Pathak, J. Malik, RSS 2021, arXiv:2107.04034 [M]. Phase 1 trains a base policy with an encoder of privileged env factors (friction, mass, motor strength, etc.) into a latent "extrinsics" vector. Phase 2 trains, by supervised regression in sim, an adaptation module that predicts that latent from a window of recent state-action history (on the order of 50 steps). The policy is deployed zero-shot. Relevance: the canonical "teacher with privileged DR params → student from history" template. Your asymmetric critic already uses privileged DR values, so an RMA-style phase 2 is a natural extension beyond a raw 4-step history. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034)
- **In-Hand Object Rotation via Rapid Motor Adaptation (HORA)**: H. Qi, A. Kumar, R. Calandra, Y. Ma, J. Malik, CoRL 2022 (PMLR v205), arXiv:2210.04887 [V]. The user's guess at the title ("Hand-object interaction") is wrong; the correct title is above. Fingertip-only in-hand z-axis rotation. The stage-1 policy uses privileged object info (including physical properties such as mass and friction; the exact list is from memory). The stage-2 adaptation module estimates object "extrinsics" online from proprioception history only. It is trained purely in sim by supervised learning and transfers zero-shot to a real Allegro hand rotating dozens of objects of varied size, shape and weight. Relevance: the closest manipulation analogue of RMA, showing that contact/object properties can be inferred from proprioception history without touch or vision. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04887); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/qi23a/qi23a.pdf); [code](https://github.com/HaozhiQi/hora)
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer of Robotic Control with Dynamics Randomization**: X.B. Peng, M. Andrychowicz, W. Zaremba, P. Abbeel, ICRA 2018, arXiv:1710.06537 [V]. Fetch-arm puck pushing with 95 randomized parameters, including puck friction [0.1, 5], link mass [0.25, 4]×, joint damping [0.2, 20]×, controller gains [0.5, 2]×, and a random action timestep Δt ≈ 0.04 s + Exp(λ), λ ∈ [125, 1000] s⁻¹. Real-robot success rates: **LSTM 0.89±0.06; FF+history 0.70±0.10; FF 0.67±0.14; FF without randomization 0.0**. Relevance: the strongest controlled evidence on *memoryless vs short-history vs recurrent* policies under friction/latency DR in manipulation. A short history alone was not enough; recurrence helped. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.06537)
- **Asymmetric Actor Critic for Image-Based Robot Learning**: L. Pinto, M. Andrychowicz, P. Welinder, W. Zaremba, P. Abbeel, RSS 2018, arXiv:1710.06542 [M]. The critic gets full simulator state while the actor gets images. Combined with DR, it transferred to a real Fetch for pick/push tasks. Relevance: the origin of your actor/critic split. Later theory notes that the original asymmetric AC has biased gradients when the actor is memoryless/partially observed, and an "informed" variant lets the critic condition on arbitrary privileged signals (Ebi et al., arXiv:2509.26000, 2025) [V, abstract-level]. — [arXiv 1710.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542); [arXiv 2509.26000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26000v2)
- **Learning Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation**: OpenAI (Andrychowicz et al.), IJRR 2020, arXiv:1808.00177 [M]. LSTM policy with extensive physics DR (friction, masses, actuator gains/backlash, action delay and timing noise). Memory was reported to help real-world performance and to allow implicit online system ID. Relevance: large-scale evidence that recurrent policies plus friction/latency DR beat memoryless ones for contact-rich hands. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00177)
- **Preparing for the Unknown: Learning a Universal Policy with Online System Identification (UP-OSI)**: W. Yu, J. Tan, C.K. Liu, G. Turk, RSS 2017, arXiv:1702.02453 [M]. The policy is conditioned on explicit dynamics parameters (e.g., friction, mass), and an OSI network regresses those parameters from a recent state-action history. Relevance: the explicit-estimation alternative to RMA's latent. You could regress friction directly from history and feed it to the actor, or use it as an auxiliary loss. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02453)
- **Teacher-student distillation**: Lee, Hwangbo, Wellhausen, Koltun, Hutter, "Learning Quadrupedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain," Science Robotics 2020, arXiv:2010.11251 [M]. A privileged teacher (terrain, friction, contact states) is distilled into a proprioceptive TCN student over a long history. Chen, Xu, Agrawal, "A System for General In-Hand Object Re-Orientation," CoRL 2021, arXiv:2111.03043 [M] applies the same teacher-student distillation to in-hand reorientation. Relevance: the standard alternative to asymmetric AC when the deployable actor needs memory. — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251); [arXiv 2111.03043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03043)

**Explicit friction estimation in manipulation**
- **Raei et al., "A Reinforcement Learning Approach to Non-Prehensile Manipulation Through Sliding,"** RA-L 2025 (venue as reported in search snippets; arXiv:2502.17221) [V-partial]. A DDPG tray-sliding policy is paired with an online friction estimator run after each action. The estimate is fed to the actor, and the paper assesses DR vs friction-inference methods for zero-shot sim-to-real. Relevance: the closest "explicit friction inference → actor input" manipulation RL paper, though it covers sliding rather than grasp/lift. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17221v1)
- **Waltersson & Karayiannidis (2025), online contact-property estimation during in-hand sliding with a parallel gripper**, arXiv:2507.06824 [V-partial]. Estimates static and Coulomb friction and contact radius from tactile force and sliding-velocity measurements, validated in sim and real. This is not RL. Relevance: shows that friction becomes identifiable mainly when sliding occurs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06824v1)

**Bimanual / squeeze-type sim-to-real RL (closest to setup (a))**
- **Lin, Sachdev, Fan, Malik, Zhu, "Sim-to-Real RL for Vision-Based Dexterous Manipulation on Humanoids,"** arXiv:2502.20396 (v2 Sep 2025; venue not stated on the page) [V]. Includes a **bimanual box-lift** with a box too large for one hand. Object and hand friction are each randomized in [0.5, 1.5], object mass in [0.03, 0.1] kg, P gains ×U(0.8, 1.1) and D gains ×U(0.7, 1.2), plus random object forces and frame/action-lag probabilities of 0.1. A real-to-sim "autotune" picks simulator physics (friction, damping) by tracking error in under 4 min. Specialists use an asymmetric actor-critic whose critic sees mass/friction scales; there is **no history/adaptation module**. Real box-lift success was 80%. Lowest-MSE autotuned parameters gave 8/10 grasp versus 0/10 for the highest-MSE parameters. Relevance: nearly your setup. Note that sim calibration mattered more than adaptation, and that friction adaptation was not studied. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20396v2)
- **Lin, Yin, Qi, Abbeel, Malik, "Twisting Lids Off with Two Hands,"** CoRL 2024 (PMLR v270), arXiv:2403.02338 [V]. Bimanual RL with zero-shot transfer. Thread friction is emulated by a "brake" link/prismatic joint pressing on the lid, combined with physical and non-physical DR. Relevance: bimanual contact-rich task where friction was handled by sim modeling plus DR, not adaptation. Exact ranges were not verified. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/lin25c.html); [project](https://toruowo.github.io/bimanual-twist)
- **Lin et al., "Bi-Touch: Bimanual Tactile Manipulation with Sim-to-Real Deep RL,"** RA-L 2023, arXiv:2307.06423 [V-partial via search snippet]. Dual-arm tactile sim-to-real RL. Reports that the policy **tended to squeeze the object** in the bi-reorienting task because of the sim-to-real gap. Relevance: squeeze-force overshoot is a known bimanual failure mode, and over-squeezing is what a friction-robust memoryless policy would do. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06423v1)
- **Kataoka et al., "Bi-Manual Block Assembly via Sim-to-Real RL,"** arXiv:2303.14870 (2023 preprint) [V]. Two xArm6 robots with parallel grippers. Ablations cover action delays and action interpolation, and each component was called "critical." Success was over 90% in sim and 50% on real hardware. The abstract gives no numbers for the delay ablation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14870v1)
- **Wang & Kasaei (2025), dual-arm grasping of large flat objects with PPO in Isaac Gym,** zero-shot transfer [search snippet only; details unverified]. — see search link [arXiv 2504.03500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03500v1)

### Inferences
- **Friction observability in a squeeze-lift:** with a stable no-slip grasp, Coulomb friction is largely *unobservable* from proprioception. It shows up only through micro-slip or when grip force nears μ·N ≈ mg/2 per side. A 4-step (likely <0.2 s) history may therefore carry little friction information except during lift-off or slip events. Expect either (i) little gain from history, or (ii) gains concentrated in low-friction DR episodes. Log success and squeeze-force as functions of the sampled friction, not just the mean return.
- Peng et al. (FF+Hist 0.70 vs FF 0.67 vs LSTM 0.89) suggests short-history MLPs are a weak adaptation baseline. Arms worth adding are a longer history (e.g., 16–50 steps), an RMA-style latent from history supervised by the critic's privileged friction/mass, or a GRU actor.
- A memoryless policy trained over wide friction DR plausibly learns a conservative "squeeze hard enough for the worst-case μ" strategy (consistent with the Bi-Touch over-squeeze report). A useful metric is grip force relative to the minimum needed for the true μ, i.e. whether history lets the policy squeeze *less* on high-friction boxes.
- The humanoid box-lift results suggest that calibrating sim friction and gains (real-to-sim) may matter as much as adaptation. A combined design (sysID'd nominal plus DR plus history) is a reasonable baseline to report.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that studies friction **adaptation** (history, RMA or estimator) in a **bimanual squeeze/compression grasp** with **controlled real-world friction variation** (e.g., the same box with different surface coatings or plate pads). Existing bimanual work uses DR or sim calibration only. This gap is the clearest one your ablation could address.
- I found no controlled ablation of memoryless vs history actors **under an asymmetric privileged critic** on a manipulation task. The informed/unbiased asymmetric-AC line is theoretical or tested on navigation/POMDP benchmarks.
- The exact HORA privileged-parameter list and history length, and the Bi-Touch squeeze details, were not re-fetched.

## Q2. Actuator/gripper latency modeling (delay randomization, actuator nets, delay-aware MDPs, gripper delay, action-in-progress observation, slow discrete gripper)

### Takeaway
Latency handling in RL sim-to-real takes three forms: (i) randomizing action/observation delay or control timestep, which several studies identify as the single most important randomization; (ii) learning actuator dynamics (actuator nets); and (iii) delay-aware MDP formulations that augment the state with pending actions or timing information (Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht; real-time RL; concurrent control; Time-in-State RL). Your "gripper busy" bit is a minimal instance of (iii): it exposes action-in-progress state. Almost all of this literature concerns **continuous, short (ms-scale) joint-level delays**. I found no study on **slow (hundreds of ms), variable, binary gripper commands with blocking action-server semantics**. The one gripper-delay sim-to-real paper (Louette et al. 2024) randomizes the delay but does not expose it to the policy.

### Cited Findings

**Delay randomization / actuator modeling**
- **Peng et al. 2018 (above)** [V]: on a real robot, fixing the action timestep (no latency randomization) dropped LSTM success from **0.89 to 0.29**, the largest drop of any ablation. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.06537)
- **Tan, Zhang, Coumans, Iscen, Bai, Hafner, Bohez, Vanhoucke, "Sim-to-Real: Learning Agile Locomotion for Quadruped Robots,"** RSS 2018, arXiv:1804.10332 [M]. Minitaur gaits. Adds an identified actuator model and **latency** to PyBullet plus DR. Reports that accurate actuator and latency modeling was crucial for transfer. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10332)
- **Hwangbo, Lee, Dosovitskiy, Bellicoso, Tsounis, Koltun, Hutter, "Learning Agile and Dynamic Motor Skills for Legged Robots,"** Science Robotics 2019, arXiv:1901.08652 [M]. An "actuator net" (MLP over a short history of position errors and velocities) is learned from real ANYmal data to model series-elastic actuator dynamics, including their delay-like response. Relevance: the template for learning a data-driven gripper model from your measured 0.45–0.9 s logs instead of a fixed delay. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652)
- **Imai, Zhang, Wu, Yao, et al., "Vision-Guided Quadrupedal Locomotion in the Wild with Multi-Modal Delay Randomization (MMDR),"** IROS 2022, arXiv:2109.14549 [V]. Simulates per-modality latency by sampling past observations with randomized periods (e.g., a 16-frame depth buffer split into quarters, one frame drawn from each). On a real A1 in forest trials, the robot averaged 992.5 steps with MMDR versus 733.8 for No-Delay and 572.4 for Frame-Extract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14549.pdf); [project](https://mehooz.github.io/mmdr-wild)
- **Sandha, Garcia, Balaji, Anwar, Srivastava, "Sim2Real Transfer for Deep RL with Stochastic State Transition Delays,"** CoRL 2020 (PMLR v155) [V]. Shows that policies degrade under varying sensing-to-actuation delays **even with state-of-the-art domain randomization**. Proposes **Time-in-State RL (TSRL)**, which adds the delay and sampling rate to the observation, evaluated on HalfCheetah, Ant and a 1/18-scale real car. Relevance: the closest published analogue of your "busy bit", since it exposes timing state to the policy instead of only randomizing it. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/sandha21a.html); [code](https://github.com/nesl/Time-in-State-RL)
- **DeXtreme (Handa et al.), ICRA 2023, arXiv:2210.13702** [M]: randomizes action latency/delay among many other parameters for Allegro in-hand reorientation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13702)

**Delay-aware MDP / concurrent control**
- **Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht, "Markov Decision Processes with Delays and Asynchronous Cost Collection,"** IEEE Trans. Automatic Control 2003 [M]. A constant-delay MDP becomes Markov again when the state is augmented with the last observation plus the buffer of pending (sent but not yet applied) actions. Relevance: the theoretical justification for your busy bit. Without it, the gripper subsystem is non-Markov from the policy's view, and the bit is a 1-bit sufficient summary for a binary action already in flight.
- **Walsh, Nouri, Li, Littman, "Learning and Planning in Environments with Delayed Feedback,"** JAAMAS 2009 [M]: model-based handling of constant observation/action delay.
- **Ramstedt & Pal, "Real-Time Reinforcement Learning,"** NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1911.04448 [V]. Argues that standard MDPs assume the state is frozen during action selection. Defines an RTMDP where the agent has exactly one step to act (the state includes the previous action) and proposes Real-Time Actor-Critic (on SAC), which beats SAC in both real-time and turn-based settings. — [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8571-real-time-reinforcement-learning); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04448v2)
- **Xiao, Jang, Kalashnikov, Levine, Ibarz, Hausman, Herzog, "Thinking While Moving: Deep RL with Concurrent Control,"** ICLR 2020, arXiv:2004.06089 [V]. Selects actions while the previous action is still executing. Derives continuous-time Bellman equations discretized with system delays and adds a small architectural extension to value-based deep RL. Evaluated on sim benchmarks and large-scale real robotic grasping (QT-Opt style). From memory [M]: the concurrent agent is given the previous action, the time at which the action was selected, and a "vector-to-go" (remaining portion of the previous action), and secondary sources report higher grasp success and shorter episodes than blocking execution. Relevance: the most direct precedent for "give the policy action-in-progress information". Your busy bit is a binary-gripper analogue of vector-to-go. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06089v2); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/thinking-while-moving-deep-reinforcement-learning-with-concurrent-control/)
- **Bouteiller, Ramstedt, Beltrame, Pal, Binas, "Reinforcement Learning with Random Delays,"** ICLR 2021 [M]: Delay-Correcting Actor-Critic (DCAC). Augments the state with the action buffer and observed delay values, and relabels trajectories for random observation/action delays.
- **Chen, Xu, Li, Liu, Zhao et al., "Delay-Aware Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Control,"** Neurocomputing 2021, arXiv:2005.05440 [M]: a delay-aware MDP with an action-buffer-augmented state plus model-based RL.

**Gripper-specific delay / discrete gripper timing**
- **Louette, Lambrechts, Ernst, Pirard, Dislaire, "RL to Improve Delta Robot Throws for Sorting Scrap Metal,"** arXiv:2406.13453 (2024 preprint) [V]. Splits the pneumatic gripper opening delay into a start delay d₁ and an opening duration d₂, measured as 10 ms and 171 ms. These are randomized in training as d₁~N(10, 2) ms and d₂~N(171, 5) ms. **The delay is not in the observation.** A TD3 sim-trained throw policy reached 82.7% real success (62/75), 48 ms faster than pick-and-place. The paper explicitly says gripper jaw friction and opening delay are hard to model analytically. Relevance: the only paper found that models gripper actuation delay for sim-to-real. Its timing scale (~0.18 s, low variance) is much smaller than yours (0.45–0.9 s, high variance), and it does not test observing gripper state. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13453v2)
- **Jia & Chen, "Time-Aware Policy Learning for Adaptive and Punctual Robot Control,"** arXiv:2511.07654 (Nov 2025 preprint) [V]. The policy is conditioned on remaining time and a time ratio. Real FR3 tests with a **binary gripper** cover cube placing/stacking, pouring and drawers. The time-aware policy finished 18–48% faster than vanilla RL at the minimum scheduled time, and cube stacking with twice the scheduled time succeeded 9/10 versus 1/10. Relevance: same robot family and binary gripper. It conditions on task time rather than gripper-in-flight state, but it is another case where adding a timing variable to the observation changes behaviour. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07654v1)
- **Kataoka et al. 2023 (above)** [V]: action delay was one of the ablated components called critical in bimanual xArm block assembly; no numbers in the abstract.
- The humanoid paper (Lin et al. 2502.20396) randomizes action-lag and frame-lag with probability 0.1 each [V]: a simple stochastic one-step lag, far from a 0.5–0.9 s gripper.

### Inferences
- **Busy bit = minimal Markov augmentation.** Under Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht and RTRL, a policy that cannot see an in-flight gripper command faces a POMDP (it cannot tell "just sent close" from "never sent"). The 2.4× learning speedup is consistent with removing that aliasing. It also matches TSRL's finding that exposing timing info helps beyond DR alone. A natural extension is a continuous "time since command" or "expected remaining time" feature (the vector-to-go analogue) instead of a bit.
- **Early "open" while the cube is ~6 cm up** is the anticipatory behaviour predicted by concurrent-control and delay-aware MDP theory: with known latency the optimal policy issues commands ahead of time. The risk is that the timing is tuned to the *sim latency distribution*. If real latency is shorter than trained (e.g., 0.45 s versus a longer sim draw), the cube drops from height. Evaluate drop height and placement error against the actual latency on each trial.
- Gripper latency in your setup is large *and* blocking (the action server waits for a result), which differs from the ms-scale joint latency in nearly all cited work. Delay randomization alone (Louette-style) would force a conservative policy, while the busy bit lets it act in open loop on the arm while the gripper is in flight.

### Gaps
- I found no paper on RL sim-to-real with a **slow (≥0.4 s), variable-latency, binary gripper with blocking action-result semantics**, nor one that ablates **observing gripper-in-progress state** (busy bit or time-since-command) in manipulation. Louette et al. randomize gripper delay but keep it unobserved. TSRL exposes delays but in locomotion/car tasks. Concurrent control exposes action progress but for the arm motion in grasping, not for a discrete gripper.
- No paper found quantifies **emergent anticipatory gripper release** and its sensitivity to the mismatch between sim and real latency distributions.
- Not verified this session: exact numeric results for Tan 2018, Hwangbo 2019, DeXtreme latency randomization, DCAC, or the concurrent-control grasp success numbers (secondary sources only).

## Q3. What gaps remain that our experiments could fill?

### Takeaway
Two publishable-scale gaps stand out. (1) A controlled study of **friction adaptation for bimanual squeeze-lift**: memoryless vs short history vs long history/RMA-latent, under an asymmetric privileged critic, with **real friction physically varied** (pads or coatings) and grip-force efficiency measured. (2) A study of **observing actuator-in-progress state for a slow discrete gripper**: busy bit vs time-since-command vs no info vs delay randomization only. It would measure learning speed, emergent anticipatory release, and robustness to the real latency distribution (0.45–0.9 s, measured).

### Cited Findings
- Bimanual sim-to-real RL so far handles friction by DR and sim calibration, not adaptation, and reports no friction-variation real tests: box lift with friction randomized in [0.5, 1.5] and autotune but no history — [Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20396v2); lid twisting with brake-joint friction emulation — [Lin et al. CoRL 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/lin25c.html); over-squeezing noted as a sim-to-real artifact — [Bi-Touch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06423v1).
- Short history was nearly useless compared to an LSTM in the main controlled manipulation comparison — [Peng et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.06537).
- Exposing timing state helps beyond DR in locomotion/car tasks — [Sandha et al. CoRL 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/sandha21a.html). Gripper delay has been modeled only as an unobserved DR variable — [Louette et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13453v2).

### Inferences
Concrete experiment suggestions derived from the gaps:
- **(a) Friction ablation arms:** MLP-0 (current); MLP-4 (planned); MLP-16/32 or a GRU; RMA-style latent from history supervised by the critic's privileged μ and mass; and optionally explicit μ regression as an auxiliary head (UP-OSI style).
  - Metrics: success vs sampled μ (binned); minimum squeeze-force margin (F·μ / (mg/2)); slip events.
  - Real test: 2–3 surface treatments (e.g., bare, tape, rubber pad), with friction measured by a tilt or pull test.
  - Expected confound: friction is weakly observable without slip, so report whether history policies use probing or slip-induced behaviour.
- **(b) Gripper latency arms:**
  - Observation: none / busy bit / time-since-command / normalized expected-remaining-time.
  - Latency: fixed vs randomized U(0.45, 0.9) s, optionally a learned latency model from logs (actuator-net style).
  - Metrics: sample efficiency (the 2.4× claim with seeds and CIs); release height and placement error; sensitivity sweep when test latency is outside the training range.
  - This directly connects the delay-aware MDP theory (Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht; RTRL; concurrent control) to a practical, under-reported manipulation detail.
- Report the busy-bit result as an instance of state augmentation for delayed MDPs, not as an ad-hoc trick. That framing places it in the literature.

### Gaps
- My search did not surface tactile-based bimanual friction adaptation or 2026 conference papers in depth. A targeted search of CoRL/ICRA 2026 proceedings for "bimanual friction adaptation" and "gripper latency" is advisable before claiming novelty.
- Venue for arXiv:2502.20396 (humanoid box-lift) and arXiv:2504.03500 (dual-arm flat objects) unconfirmed.
